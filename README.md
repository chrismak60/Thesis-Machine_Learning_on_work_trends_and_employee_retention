The following thesis explores the multidimensional phenomenon of voluntary employee turnover in the modern business environment by combining a theoretical analysis of contemporary labor trends with the practical application of Machine Learning. The research is structured into two main parts: the analysis of the Greek labor market through primary research and the development of predictive models using secondary data.

In the first part, data collected from 120 Greek white-collar workers revealed a workforce experiencing high levels of emotional exhaustion and a strong, unmet need for flexible work arrangements, with the hybrid model identified as the ideal preference. Despite positive attitudes toward emerging trends such as Job Hopping and Quiet Quitting, their actual implementation is often hindered by the need for job security or feelings of "financial entrapment".

In the second part, various Machine Learning algorithms (including SVM, Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and XGBoost) were applied to the "IBM HR Analytics" dataset. The results demonstrated that SVM and Logistic Regression (utilizing the SMOTE technique for data balancing) exhibit the highest predictive performance. The most critical factors driving turnover were identified as overtime, frequent business travel, and low hierarchical levels.

The study concludes with strategic recommendations for Human Resource Management, emphasizing the necessity of transitioning toward a human-centric, data-driven culture. Such a culture should prioritize work-life balance and active support for employee well-being as fundamental pillars for talent retention and organizational competitiveness.
